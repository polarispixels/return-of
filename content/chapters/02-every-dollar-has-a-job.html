Every dollar gets a job, and the job is set by when you may need the money. Four buckets cover it: cushion, opportunity reserve, core, and satellite.

Time horizon decides the job

Most investing mistakes are mismatches. Money needed next year sits in stocks that fall. Money meant for twenty years sits in cash that earns little. The fix is to stop asking “what should I invest in?” and ask first, “when might I need this money?”

Stocks can fall a long way and take years to recover. That is fine if you can wait. It is not fine if a bill is due. So the rule is simple: money you may need within a couple of years does not belong in stocks. It belongs somewhere steady, even if that means giving up growth.

This is return of capital applied to the whole portfolio. You decide what is safe before you decide what is ambitious.

The four buckets

Fill the buckets in order. Each one is funded before the next gets a dollar.

Cushion and opportunity reserve

The cushion is the bucket that lets everything else stay invested. With a cushion, a job loss or a car repair does not force you to sell the core at a bad moment. Its return matters little. Its safety matters a great deal.

The opportunity reserve is dry powder. It exists so that when a setup appears that fits your rules, you do not have to sell something else to act. It also gives you a calm answer when markets fall: you have cash waiting, with a plan for when to use it. Keep it separate from the cushion. If the two blur together, you will spend an emergency fund on a trade.

Core and satellite

The core is the default home for long-term money: broad, low-cost funds and simple, rules-based baskets. It asks for little attention. Chapter 6 covers it in detail. When you are unsure where a dollar belongs, the answer is the core.

The satellite is the small, active slice. It is where swing trading, growth momentum and options live. It is capped on purpose. Because the satellite is small, a bad stretch there cannot threaten the rest. The levels you unlock in Chapter 5 mostly change what the satellite is allowed to do.

The satellite also has a fallback. If you stop doing the morning routine, the active slice returns to the core. Active money needs active attention, and when attention lapses, the default takes over.

Money needed within a couple of years does not belong in stocks.

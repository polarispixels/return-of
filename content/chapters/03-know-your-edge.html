An edge is a repeatable reason your results should beat the market after costs. Where you can show one, act on it. Where you cannot, own the market through the passive core.

What an edge is

An edge is not a feeling, a good story, or one big win. It is a specific, repeatable way of choosing trades or investments that pays more than it costs, over many tries. A trader who buys breakouts with a written entry, stop and target has a method. Whether the method has an edge is a separate question, and only results can answer it.

Edges come from a few places: better information, better analysis, better discipline, lower costs, or a willingness to hold what others will not. Most of these are hard to hold for long, because other people are looking for the same thing.

Why most active effort has no edge after costs

The market is crowded with skilled, well-resourced participants. When you buy or sell, someone is on the other side, and they often have more data and faster tools. Active work also carries costs: commissions and fees where they apply, bid-ask spreads, taxes on short-term gains, and the time you spend.

So the starting assumption is humble: until shown otherwise, an active approach has no edge. Many people feel they have one because they remember their wins. Memory flatters. It keeps the winners and softens the losers. That is a reason to measure, not a reason to quit trying.

The journal is the only honest evidence

The trade journal is how you test the assumption. Every trade goes in with its reason, entry, stop, target, exit and result, and whether you followed your own rules. Over time the journal answers the questions your memory cannot.

  • Does this setup make money across many trades, or only in the ones you remember?
  • Do your wins come from the method or from a rising market?
  • Do you follow your rules, and does the result change when you do not?

This is why the Ladder promotes you on proof from the journal and not on feeling ready. The promotion rule asks for positive expectancy and at least 90% adherence to your own rules. Both come from the journal.

Expectancy is the measure

Expectancy is the average amount a trade earns over many trades. The formula is:

Expectancy = (win rate × average win) − (loss rate × average loss)

Win rate alone tells you little. A trader who wins often can still lose money if the losses are large. A trader who wins less than half the time can still do well if the wins are large and the losses are small.

Trader B wins more often and still loses money. Trader A loses more often and comes out ahead. A positive number across a large sample is the evidence you need. A handful of trades proves nothing, so the sample has to be big enough to trust.

The passive core is the default

Where you have no demonstrated edge, the sensible choice is to own the market cheaply and leave it alone. That is the job of the core: broad, low-cost index funds that capture market returns without asking you to beat anyone. It is the default for every dollar that has not earned a more active role.

Until the journal shows an edge, the passive core is the default.

Active work is allowed, in the small satellite, as a test. If the journal confirms an edge, the satellite can grow within the rules. If it does not, the money goes back to the core. This is a personal method, not advice, and it promises no result.

The Ladder puts the strategies in order. You climb one level at a time, you earn each step with evidence from your journal, and you step back down when you stop doing the work.

The four levels

Each level adds one kind of skill and one kind of risk. Nothing on a higher level is allowed until the level below it has been shown to work in your own records.

Earned with proof, not feelings

Promotion rule: positive expectancy and at least 90% adherence to your own rules, shown in the journal.

Both halves matter. Positive expectancy means that, across your recorded trades, the average trade makes money. Adherence means you followed your own written rules at least 90% of the time. A good result from sloppy trading proves nothing, because you cannot repeat it on purpose. Following the rules and still losing means the rules need work.

Feeling ready is not evidence. Confidence rises after a few wins and falls after a few losses, and neither tells you whether the method works. The journal is the only judge you cannot talk into a better mood.

Why options start from the seller's side

Level 3 comes before Level 4 on purpose. When you sell a covered call or a cash-secured put, time decay works for you, you hold the shares or the cash to back the trade, and your worst case is known in advance. You learn how volatility and assignment behave from the safer seat. When you later buy options at Level 4, you already know what the seller on the other side is thinking.

Tuition, already spent

Level 4 holds the highest-risk strategies on the menu. They get a fixed monthly tuition budget, treated as already spent. That phrase does two jobs. It caps the loss before the trade starts. It also removes the urge to chase the money back, because the money is gone the moment it enters the budget. Anything that works out is a bonus. The paper-trade gate, about 50 trades with positive expectancy, keeps you from paying tuition before you have studied.

Active management depends on activity.

Lost by neglect

Demotion rule: 10 trading days without the morning routine sends the active slice back to the core.

Active strategies need attention: stops move, options expire, setups fail. A position nobody watches is no longer managed. The rule gives lapsed attention a default. If you miss the routine for 10 trading days, the active slice goes back into the Index Core, where it needs no daily attention. Chapter 12 covers the routine and the journal in detail.

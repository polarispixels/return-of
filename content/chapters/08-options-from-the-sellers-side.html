Selling options pays you to take on an obligation you were willing to accept anyway. Covered calls and the Wheel use time decay as income, and they work in a cash account or IRA.

Options from zero

An option is a contract. One contract covers 100 shares. A call gives its buyer the right to buy 100 shares at a set price (the strike) by a set date (the expiration). A put gives its buyer the right to sell 100 shares at the strike by that date.

The buyer pays the seller a price called the premium. The seller keeps it no matter what happens. In return, the seller takes on an obligation. If you sell a call, you must sell 100 shares at the strike if the buyer asks. If you sell a put, you must buy 100 shares at the strike if the buyer asks. When the buyer does ask, that is assignment.

Time decay is why sellers like this side. An option loses value as expiration nears, other things equal. That helps the seller and hurts the buyer. Level 3 of the Ladder is built to teach this: time decay, volatility and assignment, from the side where time works for you.

Covered calls

Covered Calls (Strategy 6) means selling calls on shares you already own. The shares “cover” the obligation, so you can always deliver them. Step by step:

  1. Own at least 100 shares of something you are happy to hold.
  2. Pick a strike above the current price, and an expiration a few weeks out.
  3. Sell one call and collect the premium.
  4. If the price stays below the strike, the call expires and you keep the premium and the shares. Repeat.
  5. If the price rises above the strike, the shares may be called away at the strike.

The trade-off is capped upside. If the stock jumps far past the strike, you still sell at the strike and miss the rest. The premium is a small cushion on the way down, not insurance. Risk is low to medium and effort is medium.

Cash-secured puts and the Wheel

A cash-secured put means selling a put while holding enough cash to buy the shares if assigned. You are paid to wait for a price you already wanted. If the stock stays above the strike, the put expires and you keep the premium. If it falls below, you buy 100 shares at the strike.

Be clear about the risk. A cash-secured put takes the full downside of owning the stock, less the premium. If the stock falls sharply, you still own it at the higher strike. Only sell puts on shares you would gladly own at that price.

The Wheel (Strategy 7) is the cycle that joins the two. Sell puts until you are assigned. Once you own the shares, sell covered calls. When the shares are called away, you hold cash again, and you start over with puts. Risk is medium and effort is medium.

Why it fits the method

The cash and the shares are both set aside before the trade, so the maximum cost is known. That matches return of capital first. The same rules still apply: write the plan in the journal, size it, and only sell on stocks you would hold. In a cash account or IRA, no margin is needed.

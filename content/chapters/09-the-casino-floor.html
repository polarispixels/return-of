Long options, 0DTE scalping and day trading are the highest-risk, highest-effort items on the menu. The method allows them only behind a fixed budget and a proven record.

Three strategies, honestly described

Long Options (Strategy 10) means buying calls or puts on a thesis and holding weeks to months. Risk is high and effort is medium. The buyer can lose the entire premium. Time decay, which helped you in Chapter 8, now works against you every day. You can be right about the direction and still lose, if the move is too small or too slow.

Day Trading (Strategy 11) means buying and selling stocks within the same day. Risk is high and effort is very high. It demands your full attention during market hours, fast decisions, and strict control of your own behavior.

0DTE Scalping (Strategy 12) means trading options that expire the same day, held for seconds to minutes. Risk and effort are both very high. The options lose their value fast, and a position can go to zero within the day.

None of these is a place to build wealth on a schedule. They are skills with a price, and many people pay that price for a long time.

The tuition budget

The method treats this work as paying for an education. Set a fixed monthly tuition budget. Once it is set aside, count it as already spent. If it goes to zero, the month is over. You do not top it up, and you do not borrow from the core or from the cushion.

Treating the money as spent changes behavior. You stop trying to win it back. A loss is a lesson that was already paid for.

Unlock with proof

This is Level 4 of the Ladder, reached after six months or more. It opens only after about 50 paper trades with positive expectancy. Paper trading means practicing with simulated money. It starts back in Level 2, when you paper-trade options alongside your swing trades.

Expectancy is the average result per trade, counting wins and losses together. Positive expectancy over about 50 trades is not proof, but it is the minimum evidence the method accepts. Chapter 10 covers the math.

Real trading will differ from paper. Fills are worse, and nerves are real. Start small, even inside the budget.

The daily trade cap

Your Investment Policy Statement includes a daily trade cap. FINRA replaced the pattern day trader rule effective June 4, 2026, and brokers may phase the change in until October 20, 2027, so the old outside speed bump is going away (Chapter 14 has the details and the source). Your own cap takes its place. Set a number, write it down, and stop when you reach it. Overtrading is the usual way a tuition budget disappears.

The same demotion rule applies as everywhere in the Ladder. If the morning routine lapses for 10 trading days, the active slice returns to the core.

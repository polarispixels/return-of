Brokers and platforms are tools you rent. The strategy, the rules and the record of what you did are yours.

Strategy first, tool second

It is tempting to open an account first and let its features suggest what to do. That gets the order backwards. A platform built for options traders will nudge you toward options. An app built for easy taps will nudge you toward trading more. Define the strategy and the rules first. Then ask which tool suits which strategy.

No single tool fits every strategy. A broker that is excellent for a long-term index core may have weak charting for swing trading. A platform with deep options tools may be a poor home for a simple automatic deposit. Expect to use more than one, and expect that to be fine.

Eight dimensions for judging a tool

These dimensions give you the same questions to ask of any broker or platform.

Not every dimension carries equal weight for every strategy. Execution and order types matter a great deal for swing trading, where the stop must work as written. Portfolio construction matters most for the core. Automation matters once you reach the journal and the systematic work. The last dimension, trust, is easy to skip and costly to ignore, including the way an app is designed to push you to trade more.

Keep your own things portable

Your rules live in your Investment Policy Statement. Your history lives in your journal. Your data lives in files you control. A platform holds orders and positions, nothing more. If you can export your trades as a CSV and your rules are written on your own pages, you can replace any platform without losing the method. That is what it means to own the process and rent the tools.

The fit matrix

The eight dimensions describe a tool. The fit matrix asks the sharper question: how well does each tool suit each strategy? Every platform gets a score from 0 to 3 against each of nine strategies from the menu, the ones Levels 1 to 3 of the Ladder use, plus Systematic.

The weights say how much each strategy matters right now. They start out set for Levels 1 and 2, so the Index Core and swing trading count most. Change them as you move up the Ladder and the ranking changes with them.

How to read it

  • The weighted score rewards generalists. A platform that is good at everything outranks one that is the best at a single job. That is a property of averaging, not a verdict.
  • Read down the columns, not only across the rows. The right question is which tool is built for this strategy, and each column has its own answer.
  • No single platform wins. The result of the exercise is a small stack, one tool per job, with each job's rules and records kept portable.
  • The scores are judgments, and they age. Platforms change their features and prices. Re-score when you change levels, and date the result.
